Diagram 7 · Flow A — Land identification to planting approvalSpecification §8.1 · states per §6

Land enters the programme through a gate, not a form. The department identifies and surveys; the verification cadre's field decision is blocking; a named custodian must accept the post-plantation obligation before approval is issued. Only then is the Location ID minted and the tag issued. Rejection is a first-class outcome — it returns to the department with structured reasons, and the rejection history stays on the parcel permanently.

Diagram 8 · Flow B — Planting to survival assuranceSpecification §8.2 · census co-signature per §7.3, §9.4

Planting is recorded against the tag as a batch; survival is a measured, tag-anchored, photo-backed observation — not a submitted number. The agency that plants cannot alone record the authoritative survival census: an independent audit-cadre officer must co-sign it. A census below threshold raises casualty replacement as a new Batch ID against the same Location ID, and generates a demand signal back to the nursery.

Diagram 9 · Flow C — Audit & rectification · Flow D — Remote-sensing corroborationSpecification §8.3 · §8.4

Every audit finding is an independent second observation: the audit-cadre officer is structurally excluded from any parcel where their identity appears in the verification record, and capture is gated by a tag tap that proves presence. Remote sensing corroborates the reported record from above — but its flag is advisory only. It becomes a task for the audit cadre; a human inspection decides, and the analytics flag never alters the record.

Diagram 12 · Offline-first field operationSpecification §11

Connectivity across 240 taluks cannot be assumed. Field capture is designed as if the network does not exist: the device carries its reference data, capture runs fully offline, and every payload is signed, encrypted and queued in order on the device itself. When connectivity returns, the sync service verifies, orders by device sequence, and commits. Capture never blocks on network — a field officer in a village without coverage is never stopped from working.
