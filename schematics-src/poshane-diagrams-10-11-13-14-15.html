Role-Based Access Control — Two Axes, One Permission

Authority in the platform is never a single grant. Every permission is the intersection of role (what a person may do), geographic scope (where they may do it), and record state (when in the lifecycle the role has authority). Part (a) shows the model with a worked example; part (b) renders the full permission matrix and the separation-of-duties rules that prevent self-approval.

All access is additionally constrained by geographic scope (§9.3) and record state (§9.1). Verification Cadre and Audit Cadre are separate roles with mutually exclusive capabilities — no identity holds both against the same parcel. Nursery Operator capabilities (stock, grading, dispatch) and Volunteer advisory observations sit outside this capability set. Public access is limited to the tag landing page and the published programme dashboard.

■ Separation of duties — six structural rules that prevent self-approval (§9.4)

Verification vs auditThe cadre that verifies land and enters its parameters never audits it. Mutually exclusive roles, enforced at parcel level.
Identification vs verificationThe department identifies land; the verification cadre approves it. Never the same identity.
Execution vs assuranceThe agency that plants cannot record the authoritative survival census alone; audit-cadre co-signature is required.
Operations vs financeScope certification and utilisation sit behind a separate role and step-up authorisation.
Administration vs dataProgramme administrators configure the system; they do not enter or alter operational records.
Volunteer vs authoritativeVolunteer observations are always advisory. They may raise a flag; they can never close one.
Step-up authorisation (§9.5) — applies even to the highest-privilege accounts. Restricted modules require a second factor at the moment of access, independent of session login. Every attempt, successful or not, is logged with identity, time, and device. Access does not persist across sessions; leaving the module re-locks it.

Diagram 11 · Specification §10

The Financial Boundary — No Funds Transit the Platform

The programme's money does not flow through the platform, deliberately. The platform records IAFT's certification of scope, registers the donor's commitment, and holds tag-anchored evidence that the work was delivered. Disbursement itself moves directly from donor to implementing agency through banking channels — visibly outside the platform boundary. This removes an entire class of risk and audit obligation from the system.

What lives inside versus outside the platform (§10.1)

ConcernWhere it lives
Certification of scope of workIn the platform — IAFT certifies; recorded and published
Fund commitment by donor / CSRIn the platform as a record — the commitment is registered
Actual disbursementOutside the platform — donor to agency, direct, through banking channels
Evidence that work was deliveredIn the platform — operational records, tag-anchored
Utilisation reporting against scopeIn the platform — evidence reconciled to the certified scope
Payment instruction / settlementNever in the platform

What this buys (§10.2)

The platform holds no payment credentials and initiates no transfers. Utilisation assurance is grounded in physical evidence — tag-anchored planting and survival records — rather than in self-reported expenditure.

A donor's assurance question — "was what I funded actually delivered?" — is answered by the operational record, not by an accounting entry.

Diagram 13 · Specification §12

Audit Log & the Evidential Chain

Every state transition in the platform writes an immutable, append-only entry carrying six dimensions: what changed, who changed it, when, where, how, and why. Entries are never updated or deleted — a correction is a new entry that supersedes an earlier one, with the superseded entry still readable. Below, the record structure; beneath it, the chain of evidence that answers an external auditor without relying on anything a person merely typed into a box.

Diagram 14 · Specification §13

Deployment, Hosting & Data Ownership

The ownership position is unambiguous: KSLSA owns the programme record in full; Grobet India Agrotech hosts and operates the platform as Technology Partner; the data is resident in India; and KSLSA may export the complete record — including the audit log — in open formats, at any time and on exit. Departmental and remote-sensing sources feed the platform through read-only machine interfaces: the platform reads, and does not write into, departmental systems of record.

Diagram 15 · Specification §14

Conceptual Data Model — Parcel and Location ID as the Spine

The principal entities and their relationships, grouped by domain. Everything joins back to the spine at the centre: one Parcel holds exactly one Location ID; one Location ID is served by many physical Tag Instances over time; one Parcel receives many Batches. Survival Census is written against Parcel + Batch and Audit against Parcel; authority flows from Identity through Role Assignment to Scope; and an Audit Log Entry can attach to any entity in the model. This is the conceptual model, not a schema.
