DIAGRAM 4 · SPECIFICATION §5

Parcel Identity & the Tag Lifecycle

The spine of the data model. A Location ID is the durable identity of a place — one per parcel, for the life of the programme. A Batch ID identifies a planting event at that place; a parcel may hold several over time. Every survival observation, audit finding and maintenance record is written against the Location ID + Batch ID pair, anchored to a physical, clone-proof tag whose lifecycle runs from issue to retirement.

DIAGRAM 5 · SPECIFICATION §6

Land Parcel Lifecycle & the Approval Gate

The central state machine of the platform, enforcing principle P1. States 1–8 constitute the approval gate: the platform makes it structurally impossible to record a planting event against a parcel that has not reached state 8 — enforced in the domain service, not at the screen. Rejection is a first-class outcome with structured reasons, and a permanently attached history.

DIAGRAM 6 · SPECIFICATION §7

Data Entry Points & the Two-Cadre Segregation

Every way data enters the platform — fifteen enumerated entry points, organised by actor, with channel, tag requirement and typical payload. Field capture is governed by seven uniform integrity rules, and by the platform's principal integrity control: two separately constituted cadres, on two different applications, where whoever enters the data does not audit it.
