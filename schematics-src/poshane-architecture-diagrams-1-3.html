Diagram 1

System Context

Specification §2 — external actors and what flows across the platform boundary

The platform sits between the field, where physical work happens and evidence originates, and the command levels that direct and assure that work. Every green arrow is an entry point that must be authenticated, scoped, and logged; every navy arrow out is a scoped view, an export, or a public disclosure. The verification cadre and the audit cadre enter data as two separately constituted teams — the auditor is never the verifier of the same parcel. Funds never transit the platform: donors pay implementing agencies directly (gold dashed line), while Poshane records only the IAFT-certified scope and the tag-anchored evidence of utilisation.

Diagram 2

Layered Architecture

Specification §3 — six layers; each layer may call only the layer beneath it

Six layers, and each layer may call only the layer beneath it. Layer 1 holds the six human-facing capture surfaces plus the machine interfaces for remote-sensing and departmental ingestion; every request passes through Access & Identity, where role and geographic scope are resolved before any query reaches the store. The twelve domain modules of Layer 3 own the record of fact. Analytics & Intelligence is deliberately drawn apart, dashed: it is derived and non-authoritative — it annotates and flags, and a flag becomes a task for a human in Layer 3, never a change to the record. The Scope Certification & Utilisation module is restricted (gold, locked): it sits behind step-up authorisation and holds certification and utilisation evidence only — no funds.

Diagram 3

Command Hierarchy & Escalation

Specification §4 — the geographic backbone, three command levels, and the two recorded flows between them

The programme's geography — State, 35 districts, 240 taluks, then the parcel as the unit of physical work and the batch as a planting event within it — is the backbone of both navigation and authority. Three command levels operate on that backbone with strictly scoped visibility: state sees everything aggregated and can drill to any parcel, a district sees only its own taluks and parcels, and a taluk sees only assigned parcels. Between them run two recorded flows: escalation moves upward on threshold breach — survival falling below threshold at census, audit flags not cleared on re-inspection, or a rectification left unresolved past its deadline — while direction and instruction move downward. Repeat breaches escalate from district to state command.
