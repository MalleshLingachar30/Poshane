Schematic G1

One parcel, end to end — what is owned and what is rented

Every step in the life of a single parcel, and which of them depend on a commercial supplier

The question a funder or an auditor asks is not which vendors appear anywhere in the system, but which of them the record depends on. Parcel geometry, the overlap gate, area reconciliation and the stored survival and canopy figures all run inside infrastructure in the India region. Commercial dependence is confined to two steps, both of which only affect what an officer looks at, never what the platform holds.

Schematic G2

Polygon lifecycle — from a walked boundary to a Location ID

§6 approval gate · the geometry must clear four checks before an identity is issued

A Location ID is an assertion that this piece of ground is distinct, is what the land record says it is, and is not already claimed. The four checks establish exactly that, and the identity is issued only once they clear. Both the raw track and the simplified working polygon are retained: the first is evidence, the second is the geometry the system operates on, and conflating them would mean either slow checks or a disputed boundary with no original to appeal to.

Schematic G3

The overlap gate — three verdicts from one check

§6.3 · thresholds held in reference data, tunable by KSLSA without redeployment

Overlap cannot be a yes or no. Consumer handsets are accurate to three to five metres, so two officers walking a genuinely shared boundary will produce polygons that intersect slightly, every time — reject on any intersection and the cadre will start gaming the walk. The check therefore classifies: slivers are tolerated and logged, material overlaps go to a human at district level, and near-identity is blocked outright. Percentages are always taken against the smaller parcel, because a small duplicate inside a large parent is invisible any other way.

Schematic G4

Remote-sensing corroboration — how a polygon becomes an indicator

§8.4 Flow D · the archive already exists; the polygon selects from it

Nothing is transmitted to the satellite. Sentinel-2 has been photographing that ground on a fixed schedule since 2015, whether or not the programme exists; the polygon is a crop applied to a scene that already sits in the catalog. That is precisely why the corroboration carries weight — the capture predates the claim, and is held by ESA rather than by anyone with an interest in the outcome. The platform exports both the indicator and the clipped scene into its own evidence store, so an officer in 2032 is served from Indian infrastructure rather than from a live query to a commercial supplier.

Schematic G5

What the imagery can and cannot resolve

The limits stated plainly, so no expectation is set that the system cannot meet

Resolution, revisit frequency and cost form a triangle in which only two can be had at once. What the programme needs is not sharpness but a consistent, comparable time series over the same polygon, which is a frequency problem — and the free tier solves it. Stating the limits in the specification is deliberate: an expectation that satellites will verify first-year survival, once formed in a meeting, is far more damaging than the limitation itself.

Schematic G6

Sources, residency and substitutability

§13 · which layer comes from where, what crosses the India boundary, and what replaces it if withdrawn

The dashed line is the only boundary that matters to a residency question. Everything to its left is stored and processed in India and owned by KSLSA; everything to its right supplies either a picture to look at or a computation to run, and each has a named replacement. Google is not selling the imagery — Sentinel-2 belongs to ESA and is free to anyone — which is precisely why the corroboration is defensible: the evidence is publicly retrievable and independently verifiable, rather than being whatever a supplier chose to provide.
