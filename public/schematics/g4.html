Remote-sensing corroboration — how a polygon becomes an indicator

§8.4 Flow D · the archive already exists; the polygon selects from it

Nothing is transmitted to the satellite. Sentinel-2 has been photographing that ground on a fixed schedule since 2015, whether or not the programme exists; the polygon is a crop applied to a scene that already sits in the catalog. That is precisely why the corroboration carries weight — the capture predates the claim, and is held by ESA rather than by anyone with an interest in the outcome. The platform exports both the indicator and the clipped scene into its own evidence store, so an officer in 2032 is served from Indian infrastructure rather than from a live query to a commercial supplier.
