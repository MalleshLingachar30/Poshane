Role-Based Access Control — Two Axes, One Permission

Authority in the platform is never a single grant. Every permission is the intersection of role (what a person may do), geographic scope (where they may do it), and record state (when in the lifecycle the role has authority). Part (a) shows the model with a worked example; part (b) renders the full permission matrix and the separation-of-duties rules that prevent self-approval.

All access is additionally constrained by geographic scope (§9.3) and record state (§9.1). Verification Cadre and Audit Cadre are separate roles with mutually exclusive capabilities — no identity holds both against the same parcel. Nursery Operator capabilities (stock, grading, dispatch) and Volunteer advisory observations sit outside this capability set. Public access is limited to the tag landing page and the published programme dashboard.

■ Separation of duties — six structural rules that prevent self-approval (§9.4)

Verification vs auditThe cadre that verifies land and enters its parameters never audits it. Mutually exclusive roles, enforced at parcel level.
Identification vs verificationThe department identifies land; the verification cadre approves it. Never the same identity.
Execution vs assuranceThe agency that plants cannot record the authoritative survival census alone; audit-cadre co-signature is required.
Operations vs financeScope certification and utilisation sit behind a separate role and step-up authorisation.
Administration vs dataProgramme administrators configure the system; they do not enter or alter operational records.
Volunteer vs authoritativeVolunteer observations are always advisory. They may raise a flag; they can never close one.
Step-up authorisation (§9.5) — applies even to the highest-privilege accounts. Restricted modules require a second factor at the moment of access, independent of session login. Every attempt, successful or not, is logged with identity, time, and device. Access does not persist across sessions; leaving the module re-locks it.
