Command Hierarchy & Escalation

Specification §4 — the geographic backbone, three command levels, and the two recorded flows between them

The programme's geography — State, 35 districts, 240 taluks, then the parcel as the unit of physical work and the batch as a planting event within it — is the backbone of both navigation and authority. Three command levels operate on that backbone with strictly scoped visibility: state sees everything aggregated and can drill to any parcel, a district sees only its own taluks and parcels, and a taluk sees only assigned parcels. Between them run two recorded flows: escalation moves upward on threshold breach — survival falling below threshold at census, audit flags not cleared on re-inspection, or a rectification left unresolved past its deadline — while direction and instruction move downward. Repeat breaches escalate from district to state command.
